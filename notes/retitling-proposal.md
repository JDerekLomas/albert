# Retitling proposal: parts, chapters, epigraphs

For *The Door in the Mountain* (book `door-in-the-mountain`, Albert's 2026-09-07 draft).
Written 2026-09-08 after reading all twenty-one chapters end to end. Nothing here has been
applied to the manuscript. Once a set is chosen, each change goes in as a reviewable
suggestion on the chapter's heading or the part-opener document, never as a direct write.

## Why replace them

The current titles are uneven in register. "Limb Salvage" and "El Robotico" sit next to
"The Axis Mundi" and "The Aya Huma". Several are generic ("The Steppe", "The Desert",
"The Holy Lands"). The word *door* is used three times, for the book, for Part III, and
for Chapter 13. Of the four epigraphs, only María Sabina comes from inside the book; Machado
and Hesse are off the shelf, and Part II has a `[Better quote]` placeholder.

## The principle

The book ends with its own instruction for titling:

> The Maya had called it *k'uh*. The Mongolians *hiimori*. Pohaku called it *mana*. Every
> culture had reached for the same space and found a different word.

So each chapter is titled with the one word it carried home. Some are borrowed from the
places he passed through (Om, Anda, Hiimori, K'uh), some are English words that did the
same work (Salvage, Succumb, Margin, Stoke). The mix is the book's own: "born between
worlds, between the hyper modern and the ancient."

The parts are titled by what was lost, in the order the book names them in its last pages:
"Lost tomb, Lost leg, Lost Cities, lost love." The fourth part is named for what was found
instead of what was lost, because that is the shape of the book.

Each epigraph is a text from inside that part's world, and each has been checked against
the physical page in Source Library. Links below.

## Part titles and epigraphs

### Part I. The Tomb (was: The Ground)

Chapters 1 to 6: the accident, the steppe, childhood, the wandering years, the bet, the
sacred mountain. What he is looking for is a grave he will never open.

> By Mount Burqan Qaldun, as to my life, which is only so much as a grasshopper, I was
> shielded. Every morning I shall sacrifice unto Mount Burqan. Every day I shall pray unto
> it. Let the seed of my seed observe this.
>
> — *The Secret History of the Mongols*, §103, c. 1240

Temüjin's vow after the mountain hides him from the Merkid, the scene Amaraa tells by the
fire in Chapter 2 and re-enacts on the summit in Chapter 6. "The seed of my seed" is the
blue spot on Charlie's back at the end of Part I. Source: Cleaves translation, 1982,
brackets removed. https://sourcelibrary.org/q/Bioqutze2spmVT2YpxW. Cleaves is still in
copyright (Harvard-Yenching); a four-line epigraph is ordinary fair use, but a publisher
may want it cleared or swapped for the de Rachewiltz wording.

Was: Machado, "Wanderer, there is no road." Fine, but it belongs to no part of this book.

### Part II. The Leg (was: The Fire, with `[Better quote]`)

Chapters 7 to 11: the breaking, salvage, the phantom, the jungle, Varanasi. The lesson that
repeats in every chapter is surrender: he stops resisting the pain and it changes; he stops
resisting the ketamine chaos and it turns to harmony; Bruna's word in the desert is
"Succumb"; he has to "let the version of that man die, not the man himself." Shiva names
it: "let each one die so the next could be born." The leg is "burned to ash," and the man
is not.

> As a man throweth away old garments, and putteth on new, even so the soul, having quitted
> its old mortal frames, entereth into others which are new. The weapon divideth it not,
> the fire burneth it not.
>
> — *Bhagavad Gita*, II.22–23, trans. Charles Wilkins, 1785

The first English translation of the Gita, and the copy scanned is the one in the
Bibliotheca Philosophica Hermetica. https://sourcelibrary.org/q/Bht2eFLTkttjegI5sPD

Alternate, if two Sanskrit epigraphs in one book feels like too much: Jacob Böhme, *The Way
to Regeneration*, 1622: "Human life is the hinge between light and darkness: whichever it
gives itself to, in that it burns." "Gives itself to" is Succumb. The German word for hinge
here is a door hinge. https://sourcelibrary.org/q/Bhtz105zlkl4wXdV4h7

### Part III. The Cities (was: The Door)

Chapters 12 to 18: the Lost Cities years. The layered ground of Acre, the door in the
mountain in Peru, the North Shore, Oman, the Chachapoya, the origin lake of the Maya. In
Chapter 10 a friar burns the Maya books "in a single fire" and four survive. The Popol Vuh
opens by saying why it had to be written down.

> Long ago there was the original book, written in ancient times, but it is hidden from the
> one who sees and the one who thinks.
>
> — *Popol Vuh*, preamble, as copied by Francisco Ximénez, c. 1701

The only surviving witness of the text, Ayer MS 1515 at the Newberry.
https://sourcelibrary.org/q/Bn0a1fFYMTCEUIROCi1

Was: María Sabina, "a world that is far away, nearby, and invisible." This is the one
current epigraph worth keeping somewhere; she is in Chapter 9 by name. If Albert wants her
kept, she could hold Part II instead and the Gita go, since Part II is where he reads her.

### Part IV. The Source (was: The River)

Chapters 19 to 22: Jamie's death, Cayambe, Charlie's coma, the recovery. What he finds at
the threshold, three times, is light: Jamie's "just better lighting," the light in the room
that thickens, Charlie's "white void" and "the love that things are made of."

> Thine own consciousness, shining, void, and inseparable from the Great Body of Radiance,
> hath no birth, nor death, and is the Immutable Light.
>
> — *Bardo Thödol* (The Tibetan Book of the Dead), trans. W. Y. Evans-Wentz, 1927

This is the text he reads at Jamie's bedside, in its older public-domain form.
https://sourcelibrary.org/q/BgRmOQgyaadc098FPsj

Why light and not love: the fact-check note folded into Chapter 19 makes the point that if
love is already on the table before Charlie says "the love that things are made of," his
line arrives pre-loaded. An epigraph about light lets him say it first.

Alternate, if Albert wants the word love on the title page anyway: Dante, *Paradiso*
XXXIII, the last line: "the Love that moves the sun and the other stars." His father's
stars, his mother's music. https://sourcelibrary.org/q/BhIhKjvwYYKKNa8FYTW

Was: Hesse, the river passage. It is already quoted inside Chapter 11, so it loses nothing
by leaving the title page.

## Chapter titles

Each entry: the new title, the line in the chapter it comes from, and one alternate in the
same grammar.

**1. Om** (was Rebirthday). Bruna's chant in the dirt: "It was just a sound, yet the only
thing keeping me breathing." The sound that arrives at every threshold in the book. Alt:
*Margin* (moved to 21 here).

**2. Anda** (was The Steppe). "We should be andas." "I said yes without hesitation." A
brotherhood beyond blood, which is what the trip found instead of a tomb. Alt: *NaChing*,
the eagle name.

**3. Yu-Min** (was Shifting Grounds). His middle name, "citizen of the universe," given so
his life "would transcend geopolitics." The chapter's last line is "Where did I belong?"
Alt: *Nocturne*, the Chopin his mother plays as his father walks out.

**4. Siddhartha** (was The Wayfarer). The book Maya puts in his hands: "I hadn't understood
it when she gave it to me. I had carried it ever since." It resurfaces in Chapters 7, 11
and 22. Alt: *Chomolungma*, where the Om medallion is pressed into his hand.

**5. Tengri** (was Burn the Ships). The eagle over Shiliin Bogd: "that was the permission
that mattered most. The older kind, the kind no ministry can grant." Alt: *Ocharo*, the
cheer to the mountain. Burn the Ships is the best of the current titles and would survive
if one had to.

**6. Hiimori** (was The Sacred Mountain). The wind-horse in the soul banner on the summit,
"the vital energy source that gave its owner strength and wisdom." Chapter 7 opens by
saying "I had felt the hiimori." Alt: *Sulde*, the soul banner.

**7. Crescendo** (was The Breaking). "Each strike of the hammer seemed to come faster by
the day, pulling me toward a crescendo as it had on the mountain." The chapter is built as
drumbeats. Alt: *Boom*, the drum hit itself, if Albert wants it blunter.

**8. Salvage** (was Limb Salvage). "Not rescue. Not recovery. Salvage." The text already
does the title's work; the adjective was padding.

**9. Succumb** (was The Ghost). Bruna's one word in Death Valley, and the turn of the whole
part. He drinks the San Pedro in Chapter 13 with the same word. Alt: *Phantom*.

**10. Ground Truth** (was El Robotico). "What was left was to walk in and ground truth it."
He ground-truths the LiDAR and his own body in the same walk. Alt: *Wahy*, the companion
self "that usually comes out at night."

**11. Shiva** (was The Sound of the River). "Shiva takes many forms. The destroyer. The
creator." The fire that has burned three thousand years; "fire is also a forge." Alt:
*Diwali*, the festival he arrives into, "light's triumph over darkness."

**12. Strata** (was The Holy Lands). "History was strata underfoot." Acre, Petra, the Dead
Sea, Santorini, Stonehenge: a chapter of layers. Alt: *Giants*, "Every culture has a giant."

**13. Pachamama** (was The Door in the Mountain). "Not Earth Mother. Earth-and-Time
Mother," in "a language that never split when from where." The book keeps its title; the
chapter no longer repeats it. Alt: *El Cóndor Pasa*, the melody he recognises in the cave.

**15. Mana** (was Heʻe Nalu, Wave Riders). Pohaku's word, "the power felt in the lava
itself," and one of the three words the book ends on. Alt: *Aloha*, "the love emerging from
everything," which is where the chapter lands.

**16. Habibi** (was The Desert). What the Bedouin called Charlie. A chapter about the
children, a divorced family on the same adventure, and a camel born in the sand. Alt:
*Magan*, the Sumerian name for Oman.

**17. Ethnosphere** (was The Cloud Warriors). Wade Davis's word, defined at the top of the
chapter and embodied by Mama Rosa at the bread oven. Alt: *Kuélap*, whose ending "lap"
means stronghold, "a clue left without context on modern maps."

**18. K'uh** (was The Axis Mundi). "The energy force that ran through all things." Noh
k'uh, Place of k'uh. The first of the three words in the final paragraph. Alt: *Matwil*,
the place of origins.

**19. Shadowlight** (was Shadow and Light). Jamie's stage name, one word, which she lives
by at the end. Alt: *Resonance*, the name of her last show.

**20. Recuerda** (was The Aya Huma). "Remember the mountain," the one word the man says as
he puts the mask in Albert's hands. The Aya Huma already fits this system and could stay;
Recuerda is here only because the brief was to replace everything.

**21. Margin** (was The Crux). "It was the same margin I had felt during the amputation. It
was the margin Charlie would need to survive." The sliver his father described on the road
through the pines. Alt: *Tebo1*, "the first. The source."

**22. Stoke** (was The Boy and the Butterfly). Charlie's word for what he felt in the
white void, said "deadpan, innocent and unassuming." The book ends on a fish, so it ends on
joy. Alt: *Metamorphosis*.

## Two other ways to name the parts

If "The Tomb / The Leg / The Cities / The Source" is too blunt, both of these are also
built from the text.

- **Thresholds.** The Gate (the Ikh Khorig, "the sound of an old hinge giving way"), The
  Mirror (Rama's mirror box; Burning Man as the mirror of a love), The Door (Elder's), The
  River (where the book ends).
- **Sounds.** The Drum (the shaman's hammer), The Sound (Om, chased to Varanasi), The Song
  (Elder's flute, Mama Rosa's farewell), The Resonance (Jamie's show, the wolves answering).
